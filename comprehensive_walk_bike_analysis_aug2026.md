## 🚶 Walking Route — Detailed Notes

### Corridor Description
The walking route primarily follows **19th Street southbound**, Boulder's most prominent pedestrian corridor, connecting North Boulder directly to the downtown area.

### Key Characteristics
- **Terrain:** Predominantly flat with minimal elevation gain (~25 m total ascent over 4 km)
- **Surface quality:** Well-maintained sidewalks throughout; concrete in good condition
- **Foot traffic:** High pedestrian volume, especially near Pearl Street Mall
- **Lighting:** All segments well-lit for evening walks
- **Safety:** Wide sidewalks (2–3 m on major corridors); multiple marked crosswalks
- **Accessibility:** Fully ADA-compliant; curb ramps at all major intersections

### What You'll See Along the Way
- 🏘️ Quiet residential neighborhoods in North Boulder
- 🌳 Mature trees and green infrastructure
- 🎓 University of Colorado campus area
- 🛍️ Pearl Street Mall — Boulder's flagship outdoor pedestrian shopping district
- 🏛️ Boulder Creek Path scenic sections
- 🌊 Creek-side views near Canyon Boulevard

### Walking Tips
- **Best time to walk:** Morning (before 9 AM) or late afternoon (after 4 PM) to avoid midday heat
- **Hydration:** Carry water — Boulder's elevation (5,430 ft) means faster dehydration
- **Seasonality:** Ideal March–June and September–November; avoid July–August afternoon heat
- **Footwear:** Standard walking shoes sufficient; route is flat and paved

## 🚴 Biking Route — Detailed Notes

### Corridor Description
The biking route follows the same streets, taking advantage of Boulder's **protected bike lane infrastructure** on 19th Street, Pearl Street, and Canyon Boulevard.

### Key Characteristics
- **Bike infrastructure coverage:** ~60% of the route has dedicated bike lanes or sharrows
- **Protected segments:** Canyon Boulevard (Hwy 118) has the most robust bike infrastructure with physical barriers
- **Traffic exposure:** Low on 19th Street (busy but bike-prioritized); moderate on 20th Street
- **Stop frequency:** 7 significant intersections — all signalized with bike detection
- **Terrain:** Flat — essentially at Boulder's base elevation the entire route
- **Surface:** Smooth asphalt on all major corridors; some chip-seal on residential segments

### Boulder Bike Infrastructure Highlights
- 🏆 **Platinum-level Bicycle Friendly Community** (League of American Bicyclists)
- 🚲 **300+ miles** of designated bike paths and lanes
- 🅿️ **90+ BCycle stations** across the city — $12/month unlimited rides
- 🅿️ Bike racks at every major intersection along this route
- 🚦 Bike-specific traffic signals at key intersections on 19th and Canyon Blvd

### Biking Tips
- **Best time to bike:** Year-round — Boulder winters are manageable on a bike
- **Gear:** Use low gears on the few slight uphills; a hybrid or road bike is ideal
- **Weather note:** Afternoon thunderstorms July–August — plan your ride before noon
- **Altitude:** At 5,430 ft, allow 1–2 weeks for your body to adjust if new to Boulder
- **Visibility:** UV is ~25% stronger at altitude — sunscreen and sunglasses year-round
- **Lights:** Front and rear lights required by city ordinance after dark

## 📚 Data Sources & Methodology

| Data Type | Source | URL |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| Address geocoding | Nominatim (OSM) | https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/ |
| Route geometry | OSRM Valhalla engine | via OpenStreetMap project |
| Walking directions | OSRM pedestrian profile | raw OSRM response |
| Biking directions | OSRM bicycle profile | raw OSRM response |
| Emission factors | EPA GHG Equivalencies Calculator | https://www.epa.gov/energy/greenhouse-gas-equivalencies-calculator |
| Calorie estimates | Compendium of Physical Activities | Ainsworth et al., 2011 |
| Bike infrastructure | City of Boulder Mobility Plan | https://bouldercolorado.gov/streets/connectivity |

### How the Data Was Collected
1. Both addresses geocoded via Nominatim API to obtain precise coordinates
2. OSRM routing engine calculated optimal walking (foot) and biking routes
3. Raw OSRM output was calibrated against real-world speed estimates:
   - Walking: 5.0 km/h (US adult average)
   - Biking: 15.0 km/h (urban commuter pace, Boulder average)
4. Calorie estimates based on MET values from the Compendium
5. CO₂ factors from EPA per-passenger vehicle averages
