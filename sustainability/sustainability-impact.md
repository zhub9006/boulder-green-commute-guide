# The Impact & Sustainability of Green Commuting in Boulder
## Why Walking and Biking Matter for Our City

> Boulder, Colorado, has long been a national leader in sustainability. This document explores what choosing to walk or bike actually means for you, your community, and the planet.

---

## 🌎 The Global Context: Transportation & Climate Change

Transportation accounts for approximately **27% of total U.S. greenhouse gas emissions**, making it the **largest contributing sector** to climate change in the country. A single-occupancy car trip of 4 km produces approximately **0.9 kg of CO₂**.

In Boulder specifically, transportation is responsible for approximately **50% of the city's total carbon footprint**, according to the City of Boulder's Climate Action Plan. **If every Boulder resident with a 4 km commute switched from driving to walking or biking just once a week**, the city would eliminate thousands of metric tons of CO₂ per year.

## 🌱 The Power of an Individual Choice

When you choose **not to drive** for the 4.4 km between 45 Oak Avenue and 1800 Broadway, this is what happens:

**Per Day (2 round trips):**
- ✅ 0 kg of CO₂ emitted
- ✅ 0 liters of fuel consumed
- ✅ ~400–600 kcal burned (walking) or ~240–360 kcal burned (biking)
- ✅ $0 spent on fuel or parking

**Per Week (10 round trips):**
- ✅ ~4.5 kg of CO₂ saved
- ✅ 5–7 hours spent outdoors
- ✅ ~$5–$7 saved in gas

**Per Year (260 round trips):**
- ✅ **~235 kg of CO₂ prevented** — equivalent to **5 trees** one year of carbon absorption!
- ✅ ~1,040 kg of CO₂ across all residents on this corridor alone if 100 commuters switched
- ✅ ~14–17 trees equivalent saved from carbon sequestration
- ✅ Equivalent to avoiding **1,100 miles of driving**
- ✅ Equivalent to **powering your home for 2 months**

## 🏙️ Community Benefits Beyond Carbon

Choosing walking and biking doesn't just help the climate. Multiply the benefit across your whole community.

Community benefits:
- **Less traffic noise and air pollution** within 500m of main corridors (19th St, Canyon Blvd). Fewer cars mean less congestion and more welcoming public spaces.
- **Naturally, fewer accidents.** According to the Boulder Police Department, fewer cars means fewer accidents involving pedestrians and cyclists—reducing emergency response workload and healthcare costs.
- **Stronger connections to local businesses and neighbors.** Recent research has found that people living in walkable areas routinely report higher community trust and happiness indices. Walking or biking through your city builds an intangible sense of belonging.
- **More economically vibrant streets.** People who walk and bike spend **up to 25% more per month** at local businesses compared to those who drive past them.

### Walking vs. Biking — Which Is More Beneficial?
Both have profound impacts:
- Walking produces **higher sustained calorie burn**, promoting healthy weight management.
- Biking builds **cardiovascular fitness** at lower perceived effort (more consistency due to lower joint impact).
- Combining both for different days of the week is ideal, as it works different muscle groups and (literally) keeps your routine fresh.

## ✈ Trip Chains: Integrating With Public Transit

One common matter that prevents many people from entirely ditching their car is the need to travel beyond the ~5 km range where walking or biking becomes less practical. Boulder, however, has a solution!

### The Power of the "Bike + Bus" Combo
This is where **RTD (Regional Transportation District)** becomes your ally. Bicycles are permitted on **all RTD buses and trains**, and Boulder Bus Lines extend to Denver, the airport, and mountain towns. During peak rush hours, every single RTD bus route for Denver – Boulder has racks and space for bikes.

- **Boulder B-cycle:** 44 stations of **short-term bike share**– perfect for the "last mile" between a transit stop and your actual destination
- **CU B-cycle:** University-specific bike share system with additional stations near the CU campus section of this route
- **Full RTD pass:** A monthly pass costs ~$105, which includes the entire RTD system and non-unlimited CU B-cycle rides.

## Case Study For This Specific Route: annual and overall percentage saved

For Boulder residents commuting from the Oak Avenue / 19th Street corridor:
- **Daily cost by car:** 4.03 km × 5 days × 52 weeks = 1,048 km/yr
- Car fuel cost at 25 mpg / $3.50/gal = **$147/yr** minimum
- Car maintenance — tires, oil changes, brake pads = **~$300/yr** minimum
- Bike maintenance for same mileage = **~$50/yr** (tires, chain, brake pads)
- **Walking saves $147–$447+ per year** on commuting alone

#### What About the Cost of a New Bike?
The average commuter bike in Boulder costs around **$500–$800**, a helmet adds **$50–$100**, and a good lock plus lights are another **$70–$120**.

- A quality commuter bike **pays for itself in its first 1–2 years** compared to the Car cost of the same commute.
- That's like getting a net *discount* for making a better choice for the planet and your health.

## 🌟 Call to Action: You Are the Proof-of-Concept

Results from 2026 and beyond suggest Boulder is locked into its climate action plan. This plan calls for a **15% reduction in single-occupancy vehicle trips by 2025 and 50% by 2030**.

Definition: "modal shift" from cars to bicycling and walking — is the city's top priority. By sharing this analysis, you're empowered to share this with neighbors, coworkers, and everyone in Boulder — especially with CU Boulder students and faculty.
